Account Decision Brief

An account pack goes in. The brief comes out with the decision, the evidence, the risk of waiting, and the next customer conversation. A claim with no source is flagged, not written in.

Try a sample account Talk about the work

Three accounts. One rule.

Composites, written for this sample. Pick one. The brief only uses what is in that pack.

What the brief is for.

Account reviews bury the decision under slides. An unsupported number can still make the page. The brief forces one decision, and it refuses the number it cannot source.

The customer problem

A leader walks into a renewal or an expansion with a pack of exports and no single view of what to do this week.

The decision it forces

Review, expand, or stay in the operating rhythm. Not a summary of every chart.

What was built

A sample that reads a fixed pack and writes the decision, the evidence, the risk of waiting, and the next conversation.

How it is checked

Every line cites a row in the pack. If the field is missing, the sentence is flagged and left out of the decision.

Where it sits in the week.

Who uses it

The account owner, before a sponsor meeting or a renewal call. Not a dashboard for the whole company.

When

When the pack changes: a usage drop, a new escalation, a sponsor change, or a pull for something new.

What it replaces

The slide that restates the exports. The meeting starts from the decision, not from the charts.

What it will not do

Invent a health score, a churn probability, or a budget that nobody wrote down.

Limit. These accounts are composites. No customer data is used. The source check is a rule in the sample, not a production evaluation suite, and this page is not a claim that the brief is deployed inside a company.
